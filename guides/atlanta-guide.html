Road Trip Stop — Atlanta, GA

Capital of the New South and the largest-scale case yet of a Black business district split apart by a freeway — Sweet Auburn stood alongside Marvel's Wakanda, AMC's zombie apocalypse, and a genuinely serious DIY/hackerspace scene. Four nights, the longest stop on this leg.

✦ Pop-Culture Connections

Films Shot Here

  • Black Panther (2018) — Wakanda's core sets were built at Pinewood Atlanta Studios (Fayetteville, ~30 min south); a chase scene used the real Old Fourth Ward Skatepark on the BeltLine, walkable from Sweet Auburn.
  • Baby Driver (2017) — Edgar Wright's opening car chase runs through real downtown Atlanta streets; the Krog Street Tunnel corridor appears later.
  • The Walking Dead (2010–2022) — the pilot's iconic shot of Rick riding a horse into an abandoned downtown was filmed on closed real streets near CNN Center; most later production moved ~35 miles south to Senoia, GA ("Woodbury") — not Atlanta proper.
  • The Hunger Games: Catching Fire (2013) — Capitol and arena sequences used Atlanta Motor Speedway and other metro sets, well outside downtown.
  • Gone with the Wind (1939) — set entirely in Atlanta, premiered here in 1939, but shot almost entirely on a California backlot. See status note below.

Notable People Born Here

  • Martin Luther King Jr. — born and raised on Auburn Avenue; the MLK National Historical Park anchors Sweet Auburn.
  • Killer Mike (Michael Render) — Run the Jewels rapper and outspoken leftist political commentator; co-owns SWAG Shop barbershop in the Old Fourth Ward.
  • Margaret Mitchell — author of Gone with the Wind, buried at Oakland Cemetery. Included honestly, not admiringly — the novel is a Lost Cause romance of the Confederacy.
  • Gladys Knight — Motown legend, raised in Atlanta.
  • Black Lips — garage-punk band formed in Atlanta in 1999, part of the city's "Nu-South" scene alongside Deerhunter and The Coathangers.

⚠ Current Status Notes:
The Grim Reader Bookshop's Upper Westside storefront was slated to open summer 2026 — right around this trip's May 12–16 window. It may still be mobile-only (a converted hearse) rather than a fixed shop; check socials first.
Georgia Tech Invention Studio is a student-run academic makerspace, not a public hackerspace — confirm visitor/tour policy before counting on a walk-in.
Oakland Cemetery's theatrical Halloween tours run in October, outside this May window — the grounds and standard tours are still worthwhile in spring.
Honest gap — bookstores: the mandatory anarchist/infoshop search turned up no dedicated storefront in Atlanta proper. On Our Own Authority!, Atlanta's own autonomous radical press (anarchism, labor history, Pan-Africanism, Palestine solidarity), operates online-only, distributing through Bluestockings, Burning Books, and Atlanta Zine Fest rather than running a physical space. A city this large and this politically significant still lacking a fixed infoshop is a real data point, not a search failure.
Honest gap — DIY electronics: Category 10 carries four picks, not five. Atlanta has a genuinely serious hackerspace scene (Freeside, Decatur Makers) but no dedicated synth or boutique pedal shop comparable to Salt Lake City's Squarewave Sound turned up in research — ASW Distillery's tour fills the last slot as a whiskey-adjacent maker stop rather than padding with a weak electronics pick.

Next Steps

Deeper Dive Sweet Auburn deserves a standalone Field Notes entry: the 1906 Race Riot origin, the "richest Negro street in the world" peak, and the specific mechanics of the Downtown Connector's construction through the district are each worth their own research pass.
Political / Historical Thread Sweet Auburn is the largest and most nationally visible instance yet of the black-business-district-erasure thread running Deep Deuce (OKC) → Freedman's Town (Dallas) → here — and it's the direct MLK connection that makes it the strongest anchor of the four.
Cross-City Link Refuge Coffee Co.'s refugee-resettlement model in Clarkston is worth checking against Charlotte and Richmond's coffee scenes for a similar community-ownership story — and Freeside Atlanta's hackerspace scale is a useful yardstick for whatever DC and Philadelphia turn up.
