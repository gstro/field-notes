Road Trip Stop — Birmingham, AL

The "Magic City" built on iron and steel, and the single most concentrated civil rights landscape on this trip — 16th Street Baptist Church, Kelly Ingram Park, and the A.G. Gaston Motel all sit within a five-minute walk. Sun Ra was born here; so was Angela Davis.

✦ Pop-Culture Connections

Films Shot Here

  • 42 (2013) — Rickwood Field, the oldest standing pro ballpark in the country, doubled for several historic fields in the Jackie Robinson biopic; the Tutwiler Hotel lent a scene too.
  • 4 Little Girls (1997) — Spike Lee's documentary on the 16th Street Baptist Church bombing, shot at the church itself.
  • Stay Hungry (1976) — Arnold Schwarzenegger and Jeff Bridges, shot around the Bank for Savings Building, Mountain Brook Parkway, and the Lyric Theatre.
  • Ravagers (1979) — a post-apocalyptic gunfight staged inside the newly closed Sloss Furnaces.
  • Sword of Trust (2019) — Marc Maron comedy shot around Southside and Irondale.

Notable People Born Here

  • Angela Davis — Marxist-feminist political theorist, raised on Dynamite Hill amid ~40 unsolved Klan bombings targeting Black families.
  • Sun Ra — Afrofuturist jazz bandleader, born Herman Blount; accessed esoteric texts as a teen through the Black Masonic Lodge library.
  • Fannie Flagg — author of Fried Green Tomatoes at the Whistle Stop Cafe, set partly in present-day Birmingham.
  • Emmylou Harris — 14-time Grammy-winning country/Americana songwriter.
  • Louise Fletcher — Oscar-winning actress, Nurse Ratched in One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest.
  • Erskine Hawkins — big-band trumpeter, composer of "Tuxedo Junction," named for a Birmingham streetcar/jazz intersection.
Correction note: Big Fish (2003) is often assumed to be a Birmingham-area film given its Alabama setting, but was shot almost entirely in Wetumpka, Montgomery, Tallassee, and on Jackson Lake Island. Selma (2014) dramatizes events tied to Birmingham's civil rights struggle but was filmed around Atlanta, GA and in Selma/Montgomery, AL — not Birmingham itself.

⚠ Current Status Notes:
16th Street Baptist Church remains an active congregation with roughly 2,000 weekly attendees — guided-tour access can be affected by services and events, so confirm hours before the May 9–11, 2026 visit window.

Furnace Fest at Sloss Furnaces went dormant 2004–2018, revived 2019–2024, and announced a 2025 return after initially billing 2024 as its final run. Confirm current-year dates before planning around it.

Honest Gaps

Bookstores — no true infoshop: the mandatory anarchist/radical/infoshop search turned up no dedicated storefront in Birmingham. Burdock Book Collective (intersectional feminist, Alabama Books to Prisons program) is the closest analogue — politically organized but not anarchist-identified, matching a pattern seen elsewhere on this Southern-corridor leg where identity-politics and mutual-aid infrastructure substitutes for a classic coastal infoshop.
Record stores — thin scene: research surfaced exactly two dedicated brick-and-mortar vinyl retailers in Birmingham proper (Seasick Records, Renaissance Records). Padding to five would mean reaching for general instrument shops rather than vinyl-focused stores.
DIY electronics — thin scene: two genuine makerspaces (MAKEbhm, Red Mountain Makers) and one vintage-gear shop (Birmingham Music Exchange, technically in neighboring Irondale), but no dedicated synth shop and no second hackerspace with published open hours.
Souvenirs — thin purpose-built retail: no dedicated Birmingham shop built around locally screen-printed apparel or prints surfaced, the way Plaza District shops function in Oklahoma City. The strongest picks here cross-list from bookstores, gifts, and sights.

Next Steps

Deeper Dive Sun Ra's Birmingham years — Fess Whatley's Industrial High School music program and the Black Masonic Lodge library that gave him access to esoteric texts — is a genuinely rich standalone thread connecting the music, horror/occult, and civil-rights interests in one figure. Worth a dedicated Field Notes entry.
Political / Historical Thread The Fourth Avenue Historic District extends the trip's black-business-district-erasure thread (Deep Deuce in OKC, Freedman's Town in Dallas) but with a variant mechanism — integration-era dispersal rather than a single highway project. Worth flagging to Greg as a genuine deviation in the pattern, not noise.
Cross-City Link Red Mountain Makers sitting at 810 4th Avenue North — literally inside the Fourth Avenue Historic District — is an unplanned, organic pairing of the DIY-electronics and civil-rights threads. Worth checking whether Richmond's St. Luke Bank district (also on the black-business-district-erasure thread, not yet guided) has anything comparable nearby.
