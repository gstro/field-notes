About this guide: This is not a comprehensive survey of the US Civil Rights Trail. It covers sites that fall on or near Greg's specific Austin-to-Philadelphia route — sites you will actually pass, or pass close enough that a detour is meaningful. Each entry is keyed to the relevant city guide or leg of the drive. Sites marked En Route are between cities; all others are in the city guides.

Opening

You are driving through the geography of one of the most consequential struggles in American history. The route from Austin to Philadelphia — unremarkable on paper, a sequence of interstates through the Deep South and up the Eastern Seaboard — happens to trace the spine of Black American political life from Reconstruction through the present day. This was not planned. It cannot be coincidence, either, given the history embedded in this land.

The Texas that produced Lyndon Johnson also produced the political will to sign the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Voting Rights Act of 1965 — and the political calculation that doing so would realign American politics for a generation. Louisiana produced Lead Belly, whose 12-string guitar and field hollers were a direct ancestor of rock and roll. Mississippi produced Medgar Evers, who came back from Normandy and was denied the right to vote at gunpoint, and who chose to stay. Alabama gave the world the image of Bull Connor's fire hoses, and gave the movement four martyred girls. Georgia gave the movement its prophet. North Carolina gave it the lunch counter. Virginia gave it Maggie Lena Walker. Washington, DC gave it Howard University's law school, which gave it Thurgood Marshall, which gave it Brown v. Board of Education.

Travel this route with that knowledge active. The sites listed here are not monuments to a finished story. The Voting Rights Act Evers died working toward was gutted by the Supreme Court in 2013. The Anniston bus was firebombed in 1961; the town is on your route. The Woolworth's lunch counter in Greensboro is now a museum, but the museum had to fight to survive. None of this is settled. All of it is still in motion.
