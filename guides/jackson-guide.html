Films Shot Here

  • Hotel Tehran (2026) — Liam Neeson and Zachary Levi heist-action production filmed at Millsaps College in September 2024; the biology/chemistry building doubled as a middle school.
  • The Help (2011) — Brent's Drugs, Mayflower Cafe, and the New Capitol exterior are real Jackson locations, though most of the film used Greenwood, MS as a 1960s-Jackson stand-in.
  • Get on Up (2014) — James Brown biopic; some scenes reported filmed in Jackson (locations unconfirmed).

Correction: The Help is commonly assumed to be a full Jackson production because of its setting, but most filming used Greenwood (plus Clarksdale and Greenville) — only a handful of scenes used real Jackson locations.

Notable People Born / Raised Here

  • Eudora Welty (b. 1909) — Pulitzer Prize winner; her Belhaven home is now a National Historic Landmark museum.
  • Richard Wright — raised in Jackson 1920–25; graduated valedictorian of Smith Robertson Junior High, now a museum.
  • Cassandra Wilson (b. 1955) — Grammy-winning jazz vocalist, Jackson State class of 1980.
  • David Banner (b. 1974) — rapper/producer; testified before Congress in 2007 on hip-hop and racism.
  • Medgar Evers — based in Jackson as NAACP field secretary from 1954; assassinated here in 1963.
  • Chokwe Lumumba Sr. & Chokwe Antar Lumumba — father/son Jackson mayors rooted in Black-nationalist and Malcolm X Grassroots Movement organizing.

⚠ Farish Street status: Most of Farish Street is vacant storefronts following the 2014 collapse of the Performa Entertainment redevelopment deal. Big Apple Inn, Cathead Distillery, and F. Jones Corner are active; do not expect a bustling entertainment district.
⚠ Sunflower Oven: named as an active all-ages DIY venue in an August 2025 Mississippi Today feature, but a separate bakery directory lists it as permanently closed (it operated as both bakery and show space). Call ahead or check socials.
⚠ The Rotisserie: the restaurant Mayflower Cafe's own owner credits with inventing comeback sauce could not be confirmed as still operating. Order the sauce at Mayflower Cafe or Walker's Drive-In instead.
✦ Cross-Trip Analytical Thread — Black Business District Erasure Farish Street: CONFIRMED. By 1908 the street was roughly a third Black-owned and grew into one of the largest self-sufficient Black business districts in the country — blues labels, the Alamo Theatre, Black-owned hospitals, and Medgar Evers's NAACP field office at 507 N Farish St. It declined through the same desegregation-plus-urban-renewal mechanism as Beale Street and Deep Deuce, then suffered a second erasure when a 2002 redevelopment deal went dormant in 2014 after HUD found the project's federal funds misspent. Jackson also supplies a live counter-instance: Cooperation Jackson's worker-cooperative network, a few blocks away on W. Capitol St., explicitly builds Black economic self-determination on the same lineage. Linked instances: Oklahoma City (Deep Deuce) and Dallas (Freedman's Town).
