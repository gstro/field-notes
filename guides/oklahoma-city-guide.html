Road Trip Stop — Oklahoma City, OK

A city founded in a single afternoon by the 1889 land run and rebuilt twice since — once by urban renewal and the I-235 corridor that gutted Deep Deuce, the Black business and jazz district that produced Ralph Ellison and Charlie Christian, and once by the MAPS one-cent sales tax passed after the 1995 Murrah bombing. Clara Luper's 1958 Katz Drug Store sit-in predated Greensboro by eighteen months and is largely absent from the national account.

✦ Pop-Culture Connections

Films Shot Here

  • Twisters (2024) — downtown Park Avenue doubled for New York City; streets closed 19 hrs in May 2023, dressed with scaffolding, subway signage and taxi cabs. Prairie Surf Studios (former convention center) housed interior sets; The Gilded Acorn played the NYC coffee shop where Kate meets Javi.
  • Elizabethtown (2005) — the Survivor Tree at the OKC National Memorial appears; the elm that survived the 1995 bombing.
  • Rain Man (1988) — pivotal sequences in El Reno (also home of the fried onion burger) and Guthrie's Victorian downtown.
  • Bringing Up Bobby (2011) — Milla Jovovich, filmed partly in OKC and Arcadia.

Notable People Born / Raised Here

  • Ralph Ellison — author of Invisible Man, grew up in segregated Deep Deuce surrounded by jazz and blues, taught by bandleader Zelia N. Breaux.
  • Charlie Christian — established the electric guitar as a lead instrument; also raised in Deep Deuce and taught by Breaux.
  • Clara Luper — led the Aug 19–21, 1958 Katz Drug Store sit-in, eighteen months before Greensboro; arrested 26 times over her activist career.
  • Wayne Coyne (The Flaming Lips) — founded The Womb, a psychedelic arts center whose building now houses Factory Obscura's Mix-Tape.
  • Chat Pile — OKC sludge/noise-metal band named for NE Oklahoma mine tailings; played Tower Theatre in October 2025, inside this trip's visit window.
  • Ed Ruscha — pop artist, raised in Oklahoma City.
Correction / common misattribution: Twister (1996), August: Osage County (2013) and Killers of the Flower Moon (2023) are frequently credited to Oklahoma City but were shot elsewhere in the state — Wakita/Ponca City for the first, Pawhuska/Bartlesville/Osage County for the latter two. Only the 2024 Twisters sequel is genuinely an OKC production. — TravelOK, Tulsa City-County Library, WorldAtlas

⚠ Current Status Notes:
Clara Luper National Sit-In Plaza was dedicated November 3, 2025 — after this trip's Oct 20–22, 2025 visit window closed. The finished bronze installation would not yet have been standing during the actual stay.
Nappy Roots Books has relocated; older directory listings show 3705 N Springlake Dr, the shop's own announcement gives 3621 N Lottie Ave.
Split-T, the OKC restaurant credited with inventing the Theta burger, is closed — order at Johnnie's Charcoal Broiler or The Garage instead.
39th Street Records (2131 NW 39th St) appears as permanently closed in at least one vinyl directory while still listed elsewhere — call ahead.
Prototek OKC's public web presence has been stale for roughly a decade despite active directory listings — confirm operation before visiting.
Honest gap — Bookstores: the mandatory anarchist/radical/infoshop search returned no dedicated storefront in Oklahoma City; OKC doesn't appear in radical-guide.com's bookstore-collective directory. The nearest true infoshop is Red Scare Infoshop in Tulsa, ~100 miles east. OKC's equivalent infrastructure is cooperative rather than anarchist — Literati Press became Oklahoma's first community-owned bookshop when The Writers Co-op began taking ownership in 2025 — plus OKC DSA working groups, which organize but don't run a public space. A red-state capital produces co-ops and mutual-aid networks where a coastal city produces infoshops.
Honest gap — DIY Electronics: Category 10 carries four picks, not five. OKC has a world-class boutique electronics manufacturer in Keeley Electronics but no dedicated synth shop, no hackerspace with published public open-hours, and at least one makerspace (Prototek) whose operating status couldn't be freshly confirmed this run. Padding to five would misrepresent the city.
