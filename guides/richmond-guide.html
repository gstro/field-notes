Road Trip Stop — Richmond, VA

A former Confederate capital that spent the 2020s tearing down its own monuments, built on top of Jackson Ward — the segregation-era Black business district gutted by the same urban-renewal-plus-interstate playbook seen in Oklahoma City and Dallas.

✦ Pop-Culture Connections

Films Shot Here

  • Hannibal (2001) — The abandoned Virginia Electric and Power Co. hydroelectric plant (1904-1963) on Belle Isle appears in the film; the ruins are freely walkable today as part of the James River Park System.
  • Lincoln (2012) — Thomas Jefferson-designed capitol used for interior sequences; Richmond stood in extensively for 1860s Washington, D.C. Daniel Day-Lewis won his third Best Actor Oscar for the role.
  • Loving (2016) — Dramatization of Loving v. Virginia, the 1967 Supreme Court case that struck down interracial-marriage bans; filmed largely in central Virginia, close to the case's Caroline County origins north of Richmond.
  • Mercy Street (2016) — PBS Civil War-hospital drama set partly around Richmond's wartime medical infrastructure (echoing the real Chimborazo Hospital in Church Hill, once the Confederacy's largest military hospital).
  • TURN: Washington's Spies (2014) — Revolutionary War spy-ring series that used Richmond-area locations for its Colonial-era setting.
  • Correction: Gone with the Wind is popularly but incorrectly linked to Richmond: local legend claims the grand staircase at the Jefferson Hotel inspired Tara's staircase in the film. The hotel's staircase is real and worth seeing, but GWTW was shot on MGM soundstages in California, not in Richmond, and the 'inspiration' claim is unverified lore rather than documented production history.

Notable People Born / Raised Here

  • D'Angelo — Neo-soul pioneer, born and raised in Richmond's Southside in a Pentecostal preacher's household; died October 2025 and was buried at Richmond's Mount Calvary Cemetery after a service at Saint Paul's Baptist Church.
  • GWAR (founded by Dave Brockie and collaborators) — Formed at the Richmond Dairy, a former bottling plant turned artist space; the band's monster-costume shock-rock aesthetic runs directly through the traveler's horror and punk interests. Slave Pit Inc., the band's art/effects studio, and GWARbar in Jackson Ward keep the connection live in the city today.
  • Arthur Ashe — Tennis champion and civil-rights figure; his 1996 statue is now the sole monument remaining on Monument Avenue after the 2020-2022 removal of the Confederate statues.
  • Vince Gilligan — Creator of Breaking Bad and Better Call Saul, born in Richmond.
  • Edgar Allan Poe — Not born in Richmond (Boston) but raised there from infancy after his mother's death; his formative and most productive Richmond years are the direct subject of the Poe Museum. Do not conflate birthplace with the Richmond connection.
  • Tom Wolfe — New Journalism pioneer and novelist (The Right Stuff, The Bonfire of the Vanities), born in Richmond.
