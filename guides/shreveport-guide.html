Road Trip Stop — Shreveport, LA

A Red River town where "Hollywood South" film-incentive money sits on top of a much older performance legacy — the Louisiana Hayride's Municipal Auditorium stage is where Elvis first broke, and Lead Belly worked out his style eight miles up the road.

✦ Pop-Culture Connections

Films Shot Here

  • Battle: Los Angeles (2011) — Jonathan Liebesman's alien-invasion film shot substantially at Shreveport's Millennium Studios.
  • Olympus Has Fallen (2013) — Antoine Fuqua's White House thriller, also shot at Millennium Studios.
  • Click (2006) and The Guardian (2006) — both filmed on location around the city.
  • Premonition (2007) — Sandra Bullock thriller, filmed in Shreveport.
  • True Blood (HBO, 2008–2014) — extensively shot in Shreveport-Bossier; the Ogilvie-Wiener Mansion appeared in the opening credits during its decay years.

Notable People Born Here

  • Huddie "Lead Belly" Ledbetter — foundational blues/folk songster, raised in the district now renamed Ledbetter Heights in his honor.
  • Jericho Brown — Pulitzer Prize-winning poet (The Tradition, 2020).
  • William Joyce — children's author who founded Moonbot Studios here; its short film won the 2012 Best Animated Short Oscar.
  • Johnnie Cochran — civil rights and criminal defense attorney.
  • Mike Johnson — current U.S. Speaker of the House; included for completeness despite sitting at the opposite end of the spectrum from this guide's leftist framing.

Next Steps

Deeper Dive The Louisiana Hayride → Lead Belly → "Hollywood South" through-line is unusually rich for a two-night stop: three completely different eras of American performance history (1920s-40s blues, 1950s country radio, 2000s-10s film production) all anchored within a few miles of each other. Worth a standalone Field Notes entry.
Political / Historical Thread Ledbetter Heights (formerly St. Paul's Bottoms) is proposed as this trip's first Southern-corridor instance of the black-business-district-erasure thread, but it's a partial match, not a clean one — the district declined through fires/crime/disinvestment rather than the highway-construction/urban-renewal mechanism that erased Deep Deuce and Freedman's Town. Worth a judgment call before Jackson's Farish Street entry locks the thread in further.
Cross-City Link Orlandeaux's Cafe — the oldest continuously operating African-American family-owned restaurant in the U.S., still thriving since 1921 — is a direct counterpoint to the erasure thread rather than another casualty of it. Worth checking whether Jackson or Birmingham have a comparable still-operating institution to pair against their own instances of the thread.
