The thesis, revised

Trip 1 was not unguided — it was self-curated.

instinct vs. curation  →  self-curation vs. sourced curation

Every Trip 1 city had a 32–42 place self-made Google Maps list. Roughly half of all navigation went to listed places on both trips (47.4% vs 52.5%). What changed between methods wasn't discipline — it was content: the sourced guides filled the lists with different kinds of places, and the visits followed. The experiment is real; it just measures the source of judgment, not its presence.

Verdict: Both trips ran on lists; roughly half of all navigation went to listed places either way (47.4% vs 52.5%). Sourced guides inflated list size ~55% (252→389) while conversion dropped (47%→40%) — external sources grow the list, not the itinerary. On-ground searching fell 17% (ratio 1.19→0.99). The decisive difference is content: self-curation defaulted to sustenance (food 51, coffee 50 dominate Trip 1), while sourced curation surfaced the interest profile — books ×6.9 (11→76), museums/history ×2.9 (15→44), record stores 0→24. Outdoors was flat (14→17). The curation source changed what got curated, not how faithfully it was followed.

Methods & corrections

  • Interest categories keyword-tagged on direction-request names; a rough instrument (uninformatively-named places tag as nothing).
  • Adherence is directions-only. place_view matches were excluded as circular: browsing a saved list generates place_view entries for its items.
  • Per-item save timestamps do not exist in the Takeout export, and list files were edited during trips (mtimes fall inside visit windows), so pre-trip saves cannot be perfectly separated from in-situ saves.

Data notes

Sources: Google Takeout Maps My Activity, the Maps contributions archive, and 73 named saved lists (2,338 places).
